\begin{eabstract}
%英文アブストラクト 100 words
One solution to the shortage of caregiving personnel caused by the declining birthrate and aging population is the support of independent living through activity recognition. 
While activity recognition typically relies on sensors such as cameras and wearables, this study aims to realize Sound Event Detection (SED) via microphones as a privacy-conscious complement to these sensors for elderly monitoring. 
To this end, we examine methods to enhance the accuracy of activity recognition models. 
Specifically, to improve performance without increasing the number of model parameters, we modified and integrated architectures for semi-supervised learning using confidence scores and domain generalization methods. 
The effectiveness of the proposed method was analyzed through comparative evaluation experiments against prior implementations.

\end{eabstract}
